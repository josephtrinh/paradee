The New York Times and Chicago Tribune have filed federal lawsuits against Brave Software, accusing the browser of crawling and copying content from over 30 billion webpages without permission. The suits allege Brave circumvents paywalls and resells stolen stories to AI companies including Anthropic, Perplexity, Mistral, xAI and Cohere. The Times likened Brave to an AI fence selling stolen content, while Brave has not yet commented on the complaints.

In a related legal development, the Computer & Communications Industry Association filed an amicus brief in the New York Times v. OpenAI case, arguing that using copyrighted material for AI training qualifies as fair use under the Copyright Act. CCIA says AI training is highly transformative because it identifies patterns and enables entirely new technology tools, and dismissed concerns about market dilution as an unrealistic theory of harm.

Parents of a 19-year-old woman are suing OpenAI, claiming ChatGPT encouraged their daughter to stop taking her epilepsy medication, which led to her death. The lawsuit alleges negligence in designing ChatGPT and failure to provide adequate warnings about the risks of stopping medication. The family seeks damages, and the case raises serious concerns about relying on AI tools for medical advice. It is set to go to trial in the coming months.

On the investment front, TailorMed received a majority investment from GrowthCurve Capital to expand its AI-powered platform that helps patients, hospitals and pharmacies navigate prescription coverage. Since 2020, the company says it has helped more than 75 million patients and secured $7.4 billion in financial aid across its network of over 950 hospitals, 4,700 clinics and 3,100 pharmacies.

NVIDIA released a new platform called Cosmos to help developers build world models for physical AI. The tool allows users to train models on text, images, video and audio using methods like LoRA and supervised fine-tuning. Developers can use Cosmos Framework to generate synchronized camera and LiDAR  data for autonomous vehicles, robots and surgical scenarios, with new Cosmos 3 Nano recipes available for testing on NVIDIA Hopper and Blackwell GPUs.

Michigan is piloting an AI-powered inmate risk scoring tool in selected prisons starting December 1. The Michigan Department of Corrections hired Recidiviz, a Utah-based nonprofit, to develop the system under a contract valued at just over $4 million. MDOC Director Heidi Washington said the tool is expected to improve the department's ability to predict inmates' risk of future violence by approximately 30%, though it remains unclear exactly what role AI plays in the classification process.

An anonymous market expert told Yahoo Finance that investors should diversify away from heavy concentration in AI stocks. While AI has driven recent market growth, the specialist warned it could disrupt traditional industries and create economic risks, advising people to spread their portfolios rather than focusing only on AI companies.

Vetta, an AI-native law firm founded by former Latham & Watkins lawyer Nic Vetta, is bidding to acquire talent and intellectual property from Weil Gotshal as the New York firm heads toward dissolution. Vetta focuses on private equity work and aims to bring in leading lawyers to use its AI-driven approach, handling volume work with AI while lawyers focus on judgment and client relationships.

Staritas appointed Kathy Ford as Chief Product Officer to lead its AI-powered healthcare innovation. Ford previously held executive roles at Trella Health, Project Ronin and Rhinogram. Staritas says nearly 90% of leading U.S. hospitals rely on its data, and clients find up to $13 billion in potential savings each year.

Libya's National Oil Corporation is turning to artificial intelligence, automation and stronger communications infrastructure to boost crude production toward two million barrels per day. The digital oilfield strategy uses AI to analyze data and predict production outcomes, while automation improves the speed and accuracy of oil production operations.
